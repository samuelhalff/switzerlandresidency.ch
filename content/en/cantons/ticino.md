Ticino offers lump-sum taxation with a cantonal minimum base of CHF 434,700 (indexed, valid from 2025). Since 2021 lump-sum taxpayers here also pay cantonal and communal wealth tax, with wealth set at five times the base. It is Switzerland's Italian-speaking canton, south of the Alps.

## Ticino at a glance

Ticino lies on the southern side of the Alps, bordering Italy. The landscape runs from high alpine valleys in the north to the lakes in the south: Lake Maggiore around Locarno and Ascona, and Lake Lugano around Lugano and the Ceresio villages. Bellinzona is the capital. The Gotthard base tunnel links the canton to Zurich and central Switzerland, and Milan is close by.

Daily life is in Italian. For many families the appeal is simple: Swiss institutions with a Mediterranean pace and climate.

## Lump-sum taxation in Ticino

The federal conditions apply: no Swiss nationality, first-time Swiss tax residence or a return after ten years away, and no work in Switzerland for either spouse.

For cantonal and communal tax the base is at least CHF 434,700. Since 2021 Ticino also taxes the wealth of lump-sum taxpayers, on five times the base. The seven-times-rent test and the control calculation on Swiss-source and treaty-relieved income apply as everywhere. Federal tax is calculated separately on at least CHF 435,000 for 2026.

The regime is well used here: according to the canton, 767 lump-sum cases paid CHF 183.5 million in total tax in 2022. See [lump-sum taxation by canton](/en/guides/lump-sum-taxation-by-canton/) to compare minimums.

**Coming from Italy?** The Swiss–Italian tax treaty is one of those that require a "modified" lump sum if you want treaty relief on Italian income. Our guide to [modified lump-sum and tax treaties](/en/guides/modified-lump-sum-tax-treaties/) explains what that means.

## Residence permits

EU/EFTA nationals who do not work can settle with sufficient means and health insurance. Non-EU nationals apply as retirees (55 or over, with special ties to Switzerland) or on grounds of important fiscal interest, which needs SEM approval. We have not found an official published threshold for Ticino; figures quoted online are not confirmed by the canton, so the amount should be agreed in a ruling first.

## Inheritance and gift tax

Spouses, registered partners and direct descendants are exempt from inheritance and gift tax in Ticino. Real estate is taxed where it lies, movable assets in the canton of last domicile.

## Property

Ticino levies no real property transfer tax: buyers pay land-register and notary fees. EU/EFTA nationals living in Switzerland and C-permit holders buy freely; a non-EU B-permit holder can buy a main home where they actually live. Holiday homes for non-residents fall under the national quota and size limits, and communes above 20% second homes cannot approve new ones. A 2026 consultation proposes stricter rules for non-EU buyers; it is a proposal, not law. See [buying property and Lex Koller](/en/guides/buying-property-lex-koller/).

## Living in Ticino

People usually look at Lugano and the hillside communes around it for city services, or at the Locarno and Ascona side of Lake Maggiore for a quieter lakeside life. The public schools teach in Italian; there are also international and private options.
