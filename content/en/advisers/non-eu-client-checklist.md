Non-EU clients who do not need to work in Switzerland have two main routes: the retiree route from age 55 with special ties to Switzerland (art. 28 AIG), and a permit based on important cantonal fiscal interests (art. 30 para. 1 let. b AIG), often linked to lump-sum taxation. Both are discretionary, the canton decides and SEM must approve. There is no golden visa, and neither a company nor a property purchase creates a right to reside. Use the checklist below to screen a client before the first call.

**Key facts (as of September 2026)**

| Topic | What applies |
|---|---|
| Retiree route | Art. 28 AIG: age 55+, special personal ties, lifelong means, no work in Switzerland or abroad |
| Fiscal-interest route | Art. 30 para. 1 let. b AIG and art. 32 VZAE: centre of life in Switzerland, work only abroad |
| Approval | Canton decides at its discretion; SEM approval is mandatory for both routes |
| Investment visa | None in Swiss law |
| Lump sum, federal | Minimum base CHF 435,000 for tax year 2026; no gainful activity in Switzerland |
| Health insurance | Within 3 months of registering with the commune |
| Lex Koller | Non-EU B holders may buy a main residence at their actual domicile without a permit |

## 1. Screen the route

| Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Nationality of each person moving (and any Swiss or EU nationality) | EU/EFTA nationals follow a different, simpler regime; a Swiss dual national cannot use the lump sum |
| Age of the main applicant | The retiree route starts at 55 |
| Ties to Switzerland | Long or repeated stays, close relatives here or Swiss ancestry support the retiree route; property or business ties alone do not |
| Work plans, in Switzerland and abroad | The retiree route excludes any work; the fiscal-interest route allows work abroad only |
| Expected tax contribution | The fiscal-interest route weighs the canton's tax interest; few cantons publish a figure |
| Reputation factors | Fiscal interest is weighed against Switzerland's reputation, for example in a sanctions context |
| Centre of life | The client must actually move it to Switzerland |

**What does not work:**

- **No golden visa.** Neither the AIG nor its ordinance contains an admission ground based on an investment amount.
- **A company is not a residence right.** A company formation is assessed as self-employment under art. 19 AIG: overall economic interest, a business plan, a first permit of up to 2 years and SEM approval. It also rules out the lump sum.
- **Property is not a permit.** Owning Swiss property gives no right to reside.

For background to share with the client, see [residence for non-EU nationals](/en/guides/residence-non-eu-financially-independent/) and [is there a Swiss golden visa?](/en/guides/switzerland-golden-visa/).

## 2. Documents typically requested

Cantons set their own lists, so treat this as a starting point.

**Fiscal-interest route**

- A formal commitment not to work in Switzerland or abroad, other than managing one's own assets (Vaud)
- A copy of the lump-sum agreement with the cantonal tax authority (Vaud)
- The deed or a land-register extract, if buying (Vaud)
- For the tax side: a letter describing the situation, a proposed lump-sum amount, the lease or a property valuation, and a lifestyle form (Geneva); worldwide income and wealth and a living-cost budget (Uri)

**Retiree route**

- Evidence of the special personal ties relied on
- Evidence of sufficient means, reliably available for life
- Proof of retirement and a commitment not to work

## 3. Tax pre-assessment and ruling

- Compare **lump-sum and ordinary taxation** for this client and canton before deciding.
- Check the lump-sum conditions: not Swiss, first Swiss tax liability or after 10 years' absence, **no gainful activity in Switzerland**, and both spouses qualify.
- Estimate the base: the highest of worldwide living costs, CHF 435,000 federal minimum (2026), 7× annual rent or rental value, and the control calculation. Cantonal minimums differ.
- Check treaty relief on income from Belgium, Germany, Italy, Norway, Canada, Austria or the USA (modified lump sum).
- Prepare a **tax ruling** request: a written advance assessment by the cantonal tax authority of a concrete situation about to happen.

See [how lump-sum tax is calculated](/en/guides/how-lump-sum-tax-is-calculated/) and [tax rulings before moving](/en/guides/tax-ruling-before-moving/).

## 4. Family members

- On the retiree and fiscal-interest routes, the **spouse and minor children** may come too.
- For B holders, family members **may** join if they live together, housing is suitable, there is no social assistance or supplementary benefit, and there are local-language skills (enrolling in a course is enough at first; not required for children under 18).
- Deadlines: family reunification must be requested **within 5 years**, and **within 12 months for children over 12**. Later requests need important family reasons.
- If the spouse plans to work in Switzerland, the lump sum is lost for both spouses.

## 5. After arrival

- Register with the commune **within 14 days** of entry.
- Take out Swiss health insurance **within 3 months** of registering. If on time, cover is backdated to registration.
- The tax authority checks the lump-sum conditions every year; report any change without being asked.

See the [first 90 days checklist](/en/guides/first-90-days-checklist/).

## 6. Lex Koller status by permit

| Status | Lex Koller position |
|---|---|
| Non-EU national with a B permit | Subject, but may buy a main residence at the actual domicile without a permit |
| Non-EU national with a C permit, living in Switzerland | Not subject |
| EU/EFTA national living in Switzerland | Not subject |
| Anyone not yet resident | Subject; holiday homes only within cantonal quotas where cantonal law allows them |

Actual domicile must be proven; a permit and commune registration alone are not enough. A consultation opened on 15 April 2026 proposes that third-country nationals would need a permit even for a main residence. **It is a proposal, not law.**

## 7. Timeline factors

No official processing time is published, and we do not promise durations. What drives the timing:

- the route, and whether a tax ruling must be agreed before the permit file goes in;
- the canton's own practice and workload;
- the SEM approval step after the cantonal decision;
- how complete the file is on first submission;
- the client's own sequencing at home (sale of assets, school years, exit steps).
