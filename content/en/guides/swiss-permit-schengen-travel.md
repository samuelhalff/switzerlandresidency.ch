A Swiss residence permit (B or C) gives you the right to live in Switzerland and, for most nationalities, to travel freely within the Schengen area for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. However, it does not grant you the right to live or work in any EU country, and it is not the same as an EU residence permit. Understanding these distinctions is essential for anyone planning to move to Switzerland or travel in Europe.

**Key facts (as of October 2026)**

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Schengen travel with Swiss permit | Up to 90 days in any 180-day period (B or C permit) |
| EU residence/work rights | Not granted by a Swiss permit |
| ETIAS status | Not yet operational; launch date depends on EU timetable |
| EU/EFTA citizens' rights | Based on nationality, not Swiss permit |
| Entry/stay rules | Each Schengen state decides its own rules |
| Swiss property ownership | Does not grant residence or Schengen travel rights |

## What does a Swiss residence permit allow in the Schengen area?

A Swiss B or C residence permit allows you to travel to other Schengen countries for short stays—up to 90 days in any 180-day period—without needing a separate Schengen visa. This is for tourism, visiting family or friends, or short business trips, but not for taking up residence or employment in those countries. The rule is set by the Schengen Implementing Convention (art. 21) and is mirrored in Swiss migration guidance. Each entry and stay must comply with the local rules of the country you are visiting, and border officials in each state have the final say on entry ([SEM FAQ](https://www.sem.admin.ch/sem/en/home/themen/einreise/faq.html), v. 2026-09-30).

## Can you live or work in the EU with a Swiss residence permit?

No. A Swiss residence permit does not give you the right to live or work in any EU or EFTA country. It is a national permit, not an EU-wide right. If you wish to move to another EU state, you must apply under that country’s rules, which may include separate residence or work permits. The same applies to self-employment or remote work physically carried out in another country. Swiss authorities are clear that they can only inform about entry into Switzerland; other states decide their own rules ([SEM FAQ](https://www.sem.admin.ch/sem/en/home/themen/einreise/faq.html), v. 2026-09-30).

## Is a Swiss residence permit the same as an EU residence permit?

No, they are not the same. A Swiss residence permit is issued by Switzerland and allows you to live in Switzerland. An EU residence permit is issued by an EU member state and may grant rights to live or work in that country, and sometimes limited rights to travel or reside in other EU states. Switzerland is part of the Schengen area but not the EU, so the rights attached to its permits are more limited. EU/EFTA citizens living in Switzerland keep their own free-movement rights through their nationality, not through their Swiss permit ([SEM FAQ](https://www.sem.admin.ch/sem/en/home/themen/einreise/faq.html), v. 2026-09-30).

## Do I need a visa or ETIAS to travel in Schengen with a Swiss permit?

If you hold a valid Swiss B or C residence permit and a recognised travel document, you do not need a separate Schengen visa for short stays in other Schengen states. ETIAS (the European Travel Information and Authorisation System) is a new electronic travel authorisation for visa-exempt nationals entering Schengen, but as of October 2026 it is not yet operational. Its launch date will depend on the EU timetable. When it comes into force, requirements may vary by nationality, so always check before travel ([European Commission – ETIAS update](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/news/european-travel-authorisation-etias-will-cost-eur-20-2025-07-17_en), v. 2026-09-30).

## How does Schengen travel work for Swiss permit holders?

With a Swiss B or C permit, you can travel to other Schengen countries for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. This is a rolling window: each day you spend in the Schengen area outside Switzerland counts towards the 90-day limit. Overstaying can lead to fines, entry bans, or difficulties renewing your Swiss permit. It is your responsibility to track your days and ensure you do not exceed the limit. If you plan to stay longer or for reasons other than tourism or business, you must apply for the appropriate visa or permit from the country concerned.

For more on the different Swiss permit types and their rights, see [Swiss permits B, C and L explained for new residents](/en/guides/swiss-permits-explained/).

## What are the limits and risks of Schengen travel on a Swiss permit?

The main limits are:
- You may not stay more than 90 days in any 180-day period in the Schengen area outside Switzerland.
- You may not take up residence or employment in another Schengen country with only a Swiss permit.
- Each Schengen state can refuse entry or set additional requirements (such as proof of funds, travel insurance, or the purpose of your visit).
- Overstaying can have serious consequences, including fines, entry bans, and problems with your Swiss residence status.

Always check the entry and stay rules of the country you wish to visit, as requirements can change. For a practical checklist on your first months in Switzerland, see [Your first 90 days in Switzerland: a new-resident checklist](/en/guides/first-90-days-checklist/).

## Swiss permit, Schengen travel and family members: what to know

Family members who hold a Swiss residence permit generally have the same Schengen travel rights as the main permit holder. For non-EU/EFTA nationals, family reunification is possible for spouses and unmarried children under 18, provided certain conditions are met (shared home, suitable housing, language requirements, and no social assistance claims). Each family member must hold their own permit and travel document. For EU/EFTA nationals, the circle of family members is wider and includes dependent parents and children under 21 or dependent. For more, see [EU citizens: the Swiss B permit without work](/en/guides/eu-citizens-b-permit-without-work/).

## Comparing Swiss and EU residence permits: rights and restrictions

| Permit type | Right to live in Switzerland | Right to live in EU/EFTA | Schengen travel (90/180 days) | Right to work in EU/EFTA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Swiss B/C permit | Yes | No | Yes | No |
| EU residence permit | No | Yes (in issuing state; sometimes limited in others) | Yes | Yes (in issuing state; sometimes limited in others) |

Swiss residence permits are national documents and do not grant EU residence or work rights. EU residence permits may allow movement within the EU, but not in Switzerland unless you apply for a Swiss permit.
