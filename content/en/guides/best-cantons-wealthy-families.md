There is no single best canton for a wealthy family in Switzerland; there is the one that fits your language, schooling, tax position and pace of life. Because tax is largely cantonal, the differences are real: lump-sum minimums range from CHF 200,000 to CHF 647,100 for 2026, some cantons have abolished the lump sum altogether, and two levy no inheritance or gift tax at all. This guide lays out the cited facts side by side so you can shortlist with confidence.

**Key facts (as of September 2026)**

| | |
|---|---|
| Federal lump-sum minimum 2026 | CHF 435,000, the same in every canton for federal tax |
| No lump sum | Zurich, Schaffhausen, Appenzell Ausserrhoden, Basel-Stadt; Basel-Landschaft for the arrival year only |
| Highest cantonal minimum base | Lucerne, CHF 647,100 for 2026 |
| No inheritance or gift tax | Schwyz and Obwalden |
| No real property transfer tax | Zurich, Uri, Schwyz, Glarus, Zug, Schaffhausen, Aargau, Ticino |
| Flat-rate wealth tax | Lucerne, Uri, Schwyz, Obwalden, Nidwalden, Glarus, Appenzell Innerrhoden, St. Gallen, Thurgau |
| Spouses | Exempt from inheritance tax in every canton |

## How to think about "best"

When families ask us which canton is best, we usually turn the question around: what does your family need to be true? A few questions do most of the work.

1. **Which language do you want to live in?** French around Lake Geneva, German in Zurich and central Switzerland, Italian in Ticino.
2. **Will you be taxed on a lump sum, or ordinarily?** This depends on whether you work in Switzerland and on your nationality. If you want the lump sum, some cantons are simply not options.
3. **What matters for the next generation?** Inheritance and gift tax differs by canton, and in some cases it depends on how you were taxed.
4. **Which schools?** For many families this settles the region before anything else.
5. **City, lake or mountains?** And how close do you need to be to an international airport?

## Tax: the points that often decide it

### If you want the lump sum

Lump-sum taxation is available only to people who are not Swiss, who are coming to Switzerland for the first time or after at least ten years away, and who do not work here. If both spouses qualify, the canton choice matters: the minimums, the treatment of wealth and even the rent test differ. Vaud, for example, uses seven times rent plus 10%.

For non-EU nationals using the fiscal-interest permit route, some cantons publish their own expectations. Geneva states that the major fiscal interest amounts to an expenditure base of **CHF 750,000**. Zurich, which has no lump sum, considers fiscal interest sufficient at an annual tax of **about CHF 1 million**, confirmed by the tax authority, with close ties to the canton and the whole family relocating.

### If you will be taxed ordinarily

Wealth tax is cantonal and communal only; there is no federal wealth tax. It is mostly progressive, but nine cantons use a flat rate: Lucerne, Uri, Schwyz, Obwalden, Nidwalden, Glarus, Appenzell Innerrhoden, St. Gallen and Thurgau. Capital gains on private movable assets such as securities are tax-free across Switzerland, while gains on real estate are taxed separately by the cantons.

### Inheritance and gifts

This is where cantons differ most for families thinking in generations. Spouses and registered partners are exempt everywhere. Children are exempt in most cantons, but not in Vaud, Neuchâtel or Appenzell Innerrhoden, which apply deductions instead, and communes in Lucerne may tax them. Schwyz and Obwalden levy neither inheritance nor gift tax. In Geneva, a family taxed on a lump sum should know that the exemption for spouse and children does not apply if the deceased or donor was lump-sum taxed in one of the last three final assessments. Our guide to [inheritance and gift tax](/en/guides/inheritance-gift-tax/) explains more.

## Schools

International schools are concentrated in a few regions, which often narrows the choice quickly. Based on the schools' own websites:

- **Geneva and the Vaud shore:** the International School of Geneva (Ecolint) has campuses in Geneva, Grand-Saconnex and Founex; the International School of Lausanne is in Le Mont-sur-Lausanne; Institut Le Rosey is in Rolle; St. George's is in Montreux.
- **Vaud Alps:** Aiglon College is in Chesières, above the Rhône valley.
- **Zurich:** Zurich International School (Wädenswil and Adliswil) and Inter-Community School Zurich (Zumikon).
- **Zug:** the International School of Zug and Luzern has campuses in Baar and Hünenberg.
- **Basel region:** International School Basel has campuses in Aesch and Reinach.
- **Ticino:** TASIS is in Montagnola, above Lugano.

School details are as published on each school's own website in September 2026; please confirm curricula, ages and boarding directly with the school. Our [international schools guide](/en/guides/international-schools-switzerland/) goes region by region.

## Lifestyle, briefly

Descriptions of lifestyle are necessarily personal, so we keep this short.

- **Lake Geneva (Geneva and Vaud):** French-speaking, very international, with an international airport in Geneva and a wide choice of schools. We compare the two in [Geneva or Vaud?](/en/guides/geneva-or-vaud/)
- **Zurich:** Switzerland's largest city and financial centre, German-speaking, with a major international airport. No lump sum.
- **Zug and Schwyz:** smaller, quieter, lakeside, German-speaking, close to Zurich.
- **Lucerne and Bern:** historic towns, German-speaking (Bern is bilingual as a canton), with lakes and mountains nearby.
- **The Alps (Valais, Graubünden, parts of Vaud and Bern):** resort life, and a place many families first know through holidays.
- **Ticino:** Italian-speaking, with a southern climate and Lugano as its main city.

## A sensible way to shortlist

1. Rule out cantons that do not fit your tax route.
2. Shortlist by school region and language.
3. Compare inheritance rules if succession matters to you.
4. Visit, ideally in more than one season.
5. Confirm the numbers for your situation in a written tax ruling before you commit.
