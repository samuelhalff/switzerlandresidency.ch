Overall the site was already careful. No page claimed a "minimum tax of CHF 435,000", a 183-day rule, a golden visa, a property-to-permit link, "no inheritance tax" without qualification, a live ETIAS, or pending reforms as law. The main recurring problems were:
- **C-permit timing by nationality:** UK/US/Canada and FI/IE/LU/SE/NO/IS nationals were missing from the 5-year (discretionary) line.
- **Discretion and SEM approval left out:** the 55+ and fiscal-interest routes were shown without these conditions (mostly canton pages).
- **Permit thresholds framed as purchase prices:** Geneva's CHF 750k and Zurich's ~CHF 1m read as if they bought a permit.
- **Ticino:** the CHF 434,700 minimum was labelled "valid from 2025".
- **Smaller slips:**
  - Modified lump sum tied to nationality instead of income source.
  - Control calculation described as a minimum tax, not a floor of the base.
  - Swiss citizens abroad described as "persons abroad" under Lex Koller.
  - Geneva's lump-sum inheritance exception omitted.
  - A few FR/DE translation drifts.

Rows that say "fixed in EN/FR/DE" were corrected consistently in all three locales. Rows marked "DE only" or "FR only" were translation drift. `node scripts/validate-content.mjs` passes.

### 1. Swiss tax residence: 30/90 days, not 183 (suggested `swiss-tax-residence-rules`, category tax-and-wealth)
- Unlimited Swiss tax liability arises with a **tax domicile** (living in CH with the intention of staying permanently) or a **tax stay** of at least **30 days with gainful activity** or **90 days without**, ignoring short interruptions. [art. 3 DBG](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1991/1184_1184_1184/de#art_3); cantonal/communal tax follows the same rule (art. 3 StHG).
- Presence only to attend school or for treatment in a clinic does not create tax residence (art. 3 para. 4 DBG).
- "183 days" is not the Swiss domestic test. It shows up in treaty rules on employment income and in other countries' domestic law.
- If two states treat you as resident, the **tie-breaker in art. 4 of the applicable double tax treaty** decides (permanent home, then centre of vital interests, habitual abode, nationality). Check the actual treaty wording.
- Lump-sum link: the lump sum needs unlimited liability **for the first time or after at least 10 years' absence** (art. 14 para. 1 lit. b DBG), and that liability is defined by art. 3.

### 2. Swiss naturalisation for residents (suggested `swiss-citizenship-naturalisation`, category residence-permits)
- Ordinary naturalisation: **C permit** when you apply, plus **10 years' residence in total, 3 of them within the 5 years before applying** (art. 9 para. 1 BüG).
- Years lived in CH **between the ages of 8 and 18 count double**, but you need at least **6 years' actual residence** (art. 9 para. 2 BüG).
- What counts: time on a **B or C permit** (and FDFA legitimation cards). **F (provisional admission) counts half.** L and N permits are not listed, so they do not count (art. 33 para. 1 BüG). Living abroad for more than 6 months ends the residence period (art. 33 para. 3).
- Cantons require **2 to 5 years** in the canton (art. 18 BüG). Communes may set their own rules.
- Language: **B1 spoken and A2 written** in a national language (art. 6 BüV).
- Simplified naturalisation for the spouse of a Swiss citizen: **3 years of marriage + 5 years' residence in total, including the year immediately before applying** (art. 21 para. 1 BüG). If you live abroad: 6 years of marriage + close ties to CH (art. 21 para. 2).
- Lump-sum link: once you are Swiss, you **lose the lump sum** for the whole tax period (KS 44 §2.2), and so does your spouse (art. 14 para. 2 DBG).
- Sources: [BüG](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2016/404/de), [BüV art. 6](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2016/405/de#art_6).

### 3. Family reunification: EU/EFTA vs non-EU (suggested `family-reunification-switzerland`, category residence-permits)
- **EU/EFTA (FZA):** family members are the **spouse**, **descendants under 21 or dependent**, and **dependent ascendants** (parents, grandparents) (art. 3 para. 2 Annex I FZA). Their right depends on the main holder's right. For EU/EFTA students the circle is narrower. [SEM Weisungen VFP §7.1.2](https://www.sem.admin.ch/dam/sem/de/data/rechtsgrundlagen/weisungen/fza/weisungen-fza-d.pdf.download.pdf/weisungen-fza-d.pdf)
- **Non-EU (AIG), B holders:** spouse and unmarried children under 18 **may** join if they live together, suitable housing exists, there is no social assistance, there are language skills (enrolling in a course is enough at first), and no supplementary benefits are claimed (art. 44 AIG).
- **Deadlines (AIG):** apply **within 5 years**. **Children over 12 must be brought within 12 months.** The deadlines start when the permit is granted or the family relationship begins. Later requests need important family reasons (art. 47 AIG).
- Retiree and fiscal-interest routes: spouse and minor children may come too (art. 43/44 AIG, §2.3).

### 4. Entrepreneur / self-employed route for non-EU nationals (suggested `self-employed-permit-non-eu`, category residence-permits)
- Art. 19 AIG: a permit **may** be granted if the activity is in the **overall economic interest**, the financial and operational requirements are met, you have a sufficient independent livelihood, and quota and personal requirements are met.
- SEM looks for **lasting benefit to the Swiss labour market**: diversifying the regional economy, keeping or creating jobs for local workers, substantial investment, new orders. For start-ups, innovation and research transfer can count.
- Required: a **business plan** (activities, financing, market, staffing, investments, turnover and profit), external financing commitments, and the company-formation deed or commercial-register extract.
- First permit **max. 2 years**. It is renewed only if the business-plan targets are met (art. 62 lit. d AIG). **SEM approval** is required.
- **No statutory minimum investment or number of jobs.** Setting up a company does **not** by itself give you a right to reside.
- The 2026 quotas apply: **4,500 B + 4,000 L** for third-country nationals, and **2,100 B + 1,400 L** for UK nationals.
- The lump sum is **not compatible** with gainful activity in CH (art. 14 para. 1 lit. c DBG).
- Sources: [art. 19 AIG](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2007/758/de#art_19), [SEM Weisungen AIG Kap. 4 §4.7.2](https://www.sem.admin.ch/dam/sem/de/data/rechtsgrundlagen/weisungen/auslaender/weisungen-aug-kap4-d.pdf.download.pdf/weisungen-aig-kap4-d.pdf), [EJPD 19 Nov 2025](https://www.ejpd.admin.ch/de/newnsb/7HwBjdg5HpBA).

### 5. Schengen vs EU rights for Swiss permit holders (suggested `swiss-permit-schengen-travel`, category residence-permits)
- A Swiss B/C permit lets you travel in other Schengen states for up to **90 days in any 180-day period** (art. 21 Schengen Implementing Convention).
- It gives **no right to live or work** in an EU state. Other states decide under their own rules (SEM FAQ).
- **ETIAS** (for visa-exempt non-EU travellers entering Schengen) **is not yet operational**. The fee will be **EUR 20**, generally valid 3 years, and the start depends on the EU timetable. [SEM](https://www.sem.admin.ch/sem/en/home/overview-einreise.html)
- EU/EFTA citizens keep their own free-movement rights through their nationality, not through the Swiss permit.

### 6. C permit timing by nationality (could extend `swiss-permits-explained` instead of a new page)
- **Legal right after 5 years:** LI, BE, DK, DE, FR, GR, IT, NL, AT, PT, ES (settlement agreements).
- **Possible after 5 years (no right):** AD, FI, IE, IS, LU, MC, NO, SM, SE, VA, **UK, Canada, USA**. Integration criteria, including language, apply.
- **Otherwise 10 years** (art. 34 para. 2 AIG). **Early C after 5 years** of continuous B with good integration: **B1 spoken and A1 written** in the local national language (art. 62 VZAE).
- Sources: [SEM Weisungen AIG §0.2.1.3, §3.5](https://www.sem.admin.ch/dam/sem/de/data/rechtsgrundlagen/weisungen/auslaender/weisungen-aug-d.pdf.download.pdf/weisungen-aig-d.pdf).
